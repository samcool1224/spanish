Tense overview

El Futuro Simple

The Spanish future tense is used for actions that will happen later, predictions, and statements about what someone expects to do. In English, we often use “will” or “going to,” but Spanish usually uses one compact verb form. One helpful feature is that the future tense is built directly onto the infinitive, so once you know the endings, you can form it quickly for many verbs.

This tense is especially useful in career and workplace topics because it lets students talk about future jobs, interviews, promotions, company growth, and goals. For example, a student can say that they will apply for a position, will earn a raise, or will succeed in business.

How it works

Regular future forms are created by keeping the infinitive and adding the same endings to all verbs. The ending depends only on the subject, not on whether the verb ends in -ar, -er, or -ir.

Pronoun Ending hablar comer vivir
yo-éhablarécomeréviviré
tú-áshablaráscomerásvivirás
él, ella, usted-áhablarácomerávivirá
nosotros-emoshablaremoscomeremosviviremos
vosotros-éishablaréiscomeréisviviréis
ellos, ustedes-ánhablaráncomeránvivirán

Important irregular stems

Some very common verbs keep the same future endings but change their stem before the ending is added. This makes them look different, but the pattern is still simple once memorized.

tener → tendr-
venir → vendr-
poder → podr-
saber → sabr-
haber → habr-
querer → querr-
hacer → har-
decir → dir-
poner → pondr-
salir → saldr-

When students use the future tense

  • To describe a future action that has not happened yet.
  • To make a prediction about a career, company, or workplace event.
  • To speak about a goal, plan, or expectation in a formal way.
  • To discuss time expressions such as en el futuro, el próximo año, and dentro de diez años.

Useful notes

The future tense often sounds more formal than English. In casual speech, Spanish speakers sometimes use ir a + infinitive, but the true future tense is still important for class, reading, writing, and advanced grammar.

Examples in context
El contador ganará un aumento de sueldo el próximo año.
The accountant will earn a raise next year.
La empresa contratará a muchos aspirantes durante la primavera.
The company will hire many applicants during the spring.
En el futuro, el arquitecto diseñará edificios más sostenibles.
In the future, the architect will design more sustainable buildings.
Dentro de diez años, yo tendré una carrera estable en los negocios.
Within ten years, I will have a stable career in business.

Tense overview

El Futuro Perfecto

The future perfect tense describes an action that will have been completed before a future point in time. It combines the idea of future time with completion. In English, it is usually translated as “will have” plus a past participle.

This tense is useful when students want to describe a deadline, predict that something will already be finished, or explain that one event will be done before another future event. In a workplace setting, it can describe a report that will have been written, a resume that will have been sent, or an interview that will have already happened.

How to form it

Use the future tense of haber plus the past participle of the main verb. The participle does not change for gender or number in this tense.

Pronoun Future of haber Example with hablar Example with obtener
yohabréhabré habladohabré obtenido
túhabráshabrás habladohabrás obtenido
él, ella, ustedhabráhabrá habladohabrá obtenido
nosotroshabremoshabremos habladohabremos obtenido
vosotroshabréishabréis habladohabréis obtenido
ellos, ustedeshabránhabrán habladohabrán obtenido

Past participles

  • -ar verbs: remove -ar and add -ado, such as contratar → contratado.
  • -er and -ir verbs: remove the ending and add -ido, such as invertir → invertido.
  • Common irregulars: hecho, dicho, visto, escrito, puesto.

When to use it

  • To show that something will already be finished by a future deadline.
  • To make a logical guess about what has happened before a future moment.
  • To describe progress in projects, interviews, applications, and career planning.
  • To pair with expressions such as para mañana, para el año que viene, and cuando.

Why it matters

The future perfect helps students sound more precise and more advanced. Instead of only saying what will happen, they can say what will already be completed. That difference is important in both spoken and written Spanish.

Examples in context
Para el año que viene, el bombero habrá completado su entrenamiento.
By next year, the firefighter will have completed his training.
Cuando llegue la entrevistadora, nosotros ya habremos enviado la solicitud.
By the time the interviewer arrives, we will have already sent the application.
Dentro de diez años, muchos técnicos habrán aprendido teletrabajo.
Within ten years, many technicians will have learned telecommuting.
Para la videoconferencia, el reportero habrá escrito el anuncio.
By the videoconference, the reporter will have written the announcement.

Tense overview

El Imperfecto de Subjuntivo

The past subjunctive is used when the main clause is in the past and the dependent clause needs the subjunctive mood. It often appears after expressions of desire, doubt, emotion, necessity, or hypothetical situations. This tense is an important step toward advanced Spanish because it connects grammar, meaning, and sequence of tenses.

In a workplace context, the past subjunctive is useful for talking about what a boss wanted, what a manager expected, what was necessary at a meeting, or what an applicant should have done in the past. It is one of the most practical tenses for advanced class writing because it appears in both formal and everyday communication.

The core rule

Start with the ellos/ellas form of the preterite, remove -ron, and add the past subjunctive endings. This rule works for regular and irregular verbs alike.

Pronoun -ra ending hablaron tuvieron
yo-rahablaratuviera
tú-rashablarastuvieras
él, ella, usted-rahablaratuviera
nosotros-ramoshabláramostuviéramos
vosotros-raishablaraistuvierais
ellos, ustedes-ranhablarantuvieran

When it appears

  • After verbs of desire in the past, such as quería que and esperaba que.
  • After doubt or denial in the past, such as dudaba que and no creía que.
  • After past expressions of emotion, such as estaba contento de que.
  • After impersonal expressions of necessity or importance, such as era necesario que.
  • In hypothetical situations with si, especially when the result is conditional.
  • After como si when describing something that is not really true.

Sequence of tenses

If the main verb is present or future, Spanish usually uses the present subjunctive. If the main verb is in the preterite, imperfect, or conditional, Spanish usually uses the past subjunctive. This sequence helps the sentence stay logically consistent in time.

Examples in context
El jefe quería que la secretaria preparara el currículum antes de la entrevista.
The boss wanted the secretary to prepare the resume before the interview.
Era necesario que los aspirantes solicitaran el puesto a tiempo.
It was necessary that the applicants apply for the position on time.
Si yo fuera arquitecta, diseñaría edificios sostenibles.
If I were an architect, I would design sustainable buildings.
La jefa dudaba que el peluquero renunciara tan pronto.
The boss doubted that the hairdresser would resign so soon.
